## 1. Product Goal

Orbit Planner is not just a to-do app.

It is a calm Android productivity app that helps users connect:

Daily tasks -> Weekly missions -> Monthly focus -> Yearly goals

Main emotional goal:

Help users recover and continue without guilt.

Main differentiator:

Not a normal to-do list. A calm life-orbit command center.

Unique features:

- Year Orbit Planner
- Rescue Mode
- Energy-based planning
- Gentle reminders
- Daily closing ritual

MVP success signal:

- User can understand how today connects to the year within 10 seconds.

Research decision log:

| Decision | Reason | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep Phase 3A research-only | Protect beginner workflow and avoid premature implementation | Draft |
| Lead with recovery language | Supports the emotional goal of continuing without guilt | Draft |

## 2. Target Users

Primary users:

- Students
- Beginner developers
- Freelancers
- Busy workers
- Self-improvement users
- People who leave tasks unfinished

User problems:

- Normal to-do apps feel boring
- Pending tasks feel stressful
- Long-term goals feel disconnected from daily work
- Users forget why today's task matters
- Too many reminders feel annoying

User needs:

- Simple daily planning
- Gentle recovery for unfinished tasks
- Visual progress
- Clear long-term goal connection
- Offline-first app that works without login

Role/context prompt for survey:

- Which best describes you: student, beginner developer, freelancer, busy worker, self-improvement user, or other?

## 6. Survey Questions

Survey title:

Orbit Planner - Productivity App Survey

Survey intro:

I am designing a new Android productivity app called Orbit Planner. It helps users plan daily tasks, weekly missions, monthly focus, and yearly goals. I want your honest opinion so the app feels useful, simple, and not stressful.

Questions:

1. Which best describes you: student, beginner developer, freelancer, busy worker, self-improvement user, or other?
2. What do you currently use to manage tasks or plans?
3. What makes you stop using a to-do/planner app?
4. Do unfinished tasks make you feel stressed or guilty?
5. Would a Rescue Mode that helps move unfinished tasks feel useful?
6. How would you want an app to handle unfinished tasks?
7. Which is most important to you: daily tasks, weekly planning, monthly planning, or yearly goals?
8. Do you prefer dark calm UI or bright colorful UI?
9. What makes you return to a productivity app daily?
10. Would progress rings or visual goals motivate you?
11. Would a Year Orbit visual planner feel interesting or confusing?
12. What feature would make a planner app memorable for you?
13. How many reminders per day feel okay?
14. What should the app avoid doing?

## 8. Design Principles

1. Calm, not stressful
2. Premium, not childish
3. Recovery, not guilt
4. Visual progress, not pressure
5. Today connected to the year
6. Simple first, powerful later
7. Offline-first and beginner-friendly
8. Memorable orbit identity
9. Low cognitive load
10. No shame-based copy

Gentle copy examples:

- You have unfinished tasks. Let's rescue them.
- Move it forward.
- Still worth doing.
- Pick the next kind step.

Risk notes:

- Too many screens too early may slow the MVP.
- Year Orbit may confuse users if the connection is not obvious.
- Reminders may feel spammy if frequency is too high.
- Dark UI must remain readable in daylight.
